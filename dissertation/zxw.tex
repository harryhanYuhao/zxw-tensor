\section{Definition of ZX Calculus}

The theory of ZX calculus consists of two parts: the tensor network which is called ZX diagrams and the rewrite rules.

All bonds in ZX diagrams are 2-dimensional.
and ZX diagrams are generated by the following three classes of generators


\begin{definition}[Generators of ZX diagrams]
The $z$-spider with phase $\phi$, which we denote as $z_{\phi}$, is symmetric tensor such that $z_{\phi}(0, \cdots, 0) = 1$, $z_{\phi}(1, 1, \cdots, 1) = \exp(i \phi)$.
Hadamard gate, denoted as $H$, is a 2-legged symmetric tensor that equals to 
$\begin{pmatrix}
	1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1
\end{pmatrix}$.
An $n$-legged $x$-spider with phase $\phi$, denoted as $x_{\phi}$, is a symmetric tensor that is conjugation of $z_{\phi}$ by the hardamard gate.
\end{definition}

Since ZX calculus was invented to represent quantum circuits, most of its literature differentiates between input and output legs. 
Following the conventional bra-ket notaion where $\ket{a}$ denotes a state (column vector) and $\bra{a}$ denotes a dual state (row vector), $z,x$ and $H$ can be represented as follows:
\begin{align*}
	z_{\phi} &= \ket{0}^{\otimes n}\bra{0}^{\otimes m} + e^{i \phi} \ket{1}^{\otimes n}\bra{1}^{\otimes m} \\
	H &= \ket{0}\bra{0} + \ket{0}\bra{1} + \ket{1}\bra{0} - \ket{1}\bra{1} \\
	x_{\phi} &=  \ket{+}^{\otimes n}\bra{+}^{\otimes m} + e^{i \phi} \ket{-}^{\otimes n}\bra{-}^{\otimes m}
\end{align*}
where $\ket{+}$ and $\ket{-}$ denotes the orthonormal basis $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{0} + \ket{1})$ and $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{0} - \ket{1})$ respectively.
